The right music
for deep work

Background music either buys you focus or quietly taxes it. Which one depends on far fewer variables than the playlist industry would like you to think.

Lyrics are the whole argument

If the work involves language — writing, reading, code, anything with words in it — lyrics compete for the same machinery. This is the most robust finding in the area and the one most people ignore because they like the song.

Work that is not verbal — design, data, physical tasks — tolerates lyrics far better. If you are editing a spreadsheet, play what you like.

The question is not "is this relaxing". It is "does this ask anything of me".

Familiarity beats novelty

New music is interesting, and interesting is exactly what you do not want. A track you have heard two hundred times asks nothing. This is why a lot of people end up with one album on repeat for a year, and why that instinct is correct.

Steady beats dynamic

  • Flat dynamics. A quiet passage makes you lean in; a loud one pulls you out. Compression is your friend here.
  • No structural events. Drops, key changes and false endings all cost you a moment.
  • Consistent texture. Instruments arriving and leaving is a form of news.

Volume is not the lever you think

Slightly louder than you would choose for pleasure is often right for masking — it covers an office or a street more completely. What matters is that it stays there. A track that varies is worse at any volume than a flat one at the wrong volume.
